Constituent Statement · 2026 Mandate

A Working-Class Tech Founder’s Mandate to the Democratic Party

Why I voted Democratic to get shit moving forward: moving past political theater to real labor enforcement, safety-net modernization, and builder capital.
Author: Jayson Quindao Location: Grand Prairie, TX (DFW) Entity: Founder, GG Loop LLC
PILLAR I
Stop Criminalizing Builders in the Safety Net

I work 40+ hours every single week in systems software engineering, distributed database internals (libSQL, Qdrant), and building commercial telemetry. Yet, because revenue is in active R&D development, Texas caseworkers try to slap me with 42-hour unpaid "workfare" community service quotas—while refusing to provide a single dollar for a $33 driver's license renewal or a public transit pass.

"You cannot mandate storefront attendance when you provide zero supportive transit services under 7 CFR § 273.7(d)(4). The safety net should fund worker mobility, not weaponize administrative churn."
The Mandate: Eliminate outdated ABAWD workfare traps, protect statutory self-employment exemptions, and federally mandate that state agencies fund participant transit and licensing before issuing attendance sanctions.
PILLAR II
Hold Predatory Corporations Accountable for Wage Theft & Retaliation

At NCR Atleos / Cardtronics in Frisco, TX, my supervisor explicitly approved my remote work while sick in writing, assigned me settlement dispute tasks, and then retroactively altered chat logs to manufacture an attendance violation. I escalated with evidence to senior leadership on Oct 30, 2025; eight business days later, I was fired and had 8 hours docked from my final check. That supervisor was subsequently fired by NCR for abusive conduct.

"Working people cannot afford to spend tens of thousands on retainer fees to fight corporate retaliation. Federal labor laws must have teeth."
The Mandate: Fully fund Department of Labor (DOL) wage-and-hour enforcement, impose mandatory triple damages for retaliatory firings under the FLSA (29 U.S.C. § 215), and streamline contingency legal intake so workers can hold billion-dollar corporations accountable.
PILLAR III
Direct Capital to Solo Builders & Open-Source Innovators

Venture capital flows to bloated slide decks while solo engineers building real, deterministic code are crippled by tax code traps like the Section 174 R&D amortization rule. I built open-source civic tools like snap-worker-defense and authored critical upstream database fixes for Turso and Qdrant.

"Independent founders shouldn't be penalized for writing code. We need capital rails that reward working software, not just financial engineering."
The Mandate: Repeal the mandatory amortization of software R&D expenses under Section 174, expand federal SBIR/STTR and civic open-source technology grants (€30k+ NLnet models), and create direct public equity rails for micro-founders.
PILLAR IV
Clear the Runway for Academic Transfer & Degree Completion

I am transferring to the University of Texas at Arlington (UTA) to complete a Bachelor of Science in AI for Business. Returning students and independent adults should not be choked by institutional transcript fees, rigid credit-transfer gates, or bureaucratic delays.

The Mandate: Expand federal Pell Grants to cover career-accelerated technical degrees, mandate seamless credit transfer between public colleges, and provide tuition fee waivers for working technical operators completing state university degrees.

The Bottom Line

I cast my vote to get shit moving forward—not for symbolic speeches, but for enforceable worker rights, structural mobility funding, and capital rails for people building from the ground up. Enforce the laws, protect the builders, and clear the runway.
